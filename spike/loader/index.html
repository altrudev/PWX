What happens when you open an app

  1. The maker signs it

    Every file is fingerprinted and the list is signed with the maker's key, along with what the app may do.

  2. Your device checks it

    The signature, every file and the version are checked here, every time it opens. Anything changed is refused.

  3. It runs sealed

    It gets only what it declared, its own private storage, and no way out to anything else.

Why PWX exists

Today, using an app means trusting whoever made it, and whoever sells it to you.

A privacy policy says where your data goes, but nothing stops an app from sending it somewhere else, changing after you install it, or disappearing with your data. App stores decide what you may run and take a cut of everything sold.

PWX moves that trust to something you can check yourself. The goal: apps anyone can make, share, update and branch, that run on everything, and where you always know who made them and what they can do.

  • Private by design

    Apps declare what they may do, and the browser enforces it. Your data stays on your device.

  • Yours to keep

    An app is a signed file you own. It works offline, with no account and no store.

  • Runs on everything

    One app for phones, tablets and computers, in any modern browser.

  • Open to everyone

    Build it however you like, by hand or with AI. Anyone can branch it, and you can always see where it came from.
